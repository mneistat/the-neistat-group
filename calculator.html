Pre-Approval, Explained

The four things every Chicago buyer should understand before they tour a single property.

01

Pre-qualification vs. pre-approval

A pre-qualification is a conversation — a lender eyeballs what you tell them and offers a ballpark loan amount. Nothing is verified.

A pre-approval letter describes what a lender may be willing to lend based on its review. Ask what has been verified and which conditions remain. Final approval still depends on underwriting and the property.

02

What lenders look for

Lenders consider these factors together. Ask how your income, debts, credit and reserves affect the programs available to you.

Debt-to-income ratio (DTI) Credit score & history Reserves & liquid assets Two-year employment history
03

Documents to have ready

Gather these before your first call with a lender, then ask which additional documents your application needs.

W-2s (past two years) Federal tax returns Bank statements (60 days) Government-issued photo ID Recent pay stubs Investment & retirement statements
04

Why pre-approval wins in Chicago

Chicago's strongest listings generate multiple offers — and when they do, sellers choose the cleanest one, not simply the highest.

A written pre-approval helps a seller assess your financing readiness. Ask your lender to explain the remaining conditions before you make an offer.

Debt-to-Income, Explained

The ratio that shapes how much a lender will approve.

Debt-to-income (DTI) compares your monthly debt payments to your gross monthly income. Lenders use it to judge how large a housing payment you can carry.

Front-end ratio

Housing costs only — principal, interest, property tax, insurance, and any HOA dues — divided by gross monthly income.

Back-end ratio

Housing costs plus every other monthly debt payment: car loans, student loans, and minimum credit card payments.

A worked example

Gross monthly income
$12,000
Housing payment
$3,000
Other monthly debts
$900
Front-end ratio
25%
Back-end ratio
32.5%

A common guideline is a front-end ratio near 28% and a back-end ratio near 36%. Many lenders approve higher ratios for borrowers with strong credit or reserves, and every loan program sets its own limits — confirm yours with a lender.
